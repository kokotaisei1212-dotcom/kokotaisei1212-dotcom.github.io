The ledger

Every answer to a call with your key comes with its row and STK's signature of it. stk.py and stk.mjs keep both for you. STK keeps no row.

What a row holds

Field
vThe form of the row: 1.
ask_idThe answer's id: 32 hex.
time_msWhen STK answered, in milliseconds since 1970 (UTC).
accountYour account's id at STK. A report can be made only with a row of your own account.
conversation_idAs your code sent it: a random id your code makes for each conversation.
intentAs your code sent it.
triggerAs your code sent it: one of five words.
rule_versionThe SHA-256 of the full text of the rules that gave the answer. The rules
decisionfinish or escalate.
clauseThe numbered rule that gave the answer: 0 to 5 (0: an invoice of your account unpaid 60 days after its due date).
which_keytest or live. Only live answers are billed.
human_joinedtrue when your code said a person has joined the conversation.
kidThe key that signed the row: one or more a day (UTC); a new STK process makes a new one.

How anyone checks a row

The signature is Ed25519, over the row's fields in the order above, as JSON with no spaces. The public key of each kid is in GET https://api.stk-evidence.com/v1/rules?keys=1, no key needed. A row checks alone: no other row, no order, no secret. stk_verify.py checks a file of rows.

A key is used for one day only, and STK lets go of it when the day ends; its public key stays published, so a row of years ago is checked with its day's key.

Where the rows are kept

stk.py and stk.mjs write each row and its signature as one JSON line to stk-rows/<day>.jsonl next to your code, or give it to a function of yours (for a place where files are not kept, like a serverless function), or, when neither works, print it to standard output. An answer that arrives after your code stopped waiting is kept too.

STK cannot give a row back: it has none. Known limits

What STK keeps

GET /v1/ledger with your key: one row a day, the day's finish and escalate counts. To compare with the rows you kept. Docs
